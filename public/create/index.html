The Character Sheet

Everything your D&D character is, on one page.

Page references throughout are to the 2024 Player's Handbook.

01 · The page

What is a character sheet?

It is the one page that turns an idea in your head into someone who can actually act in the world. Who you are, what you can do, what you're carrying and what you've survived — it all lives here.

Every question at the table gets answered off this page. Can I climb that wall? How much damage did I do? Do I even know this language? You don't decide in the moment — you read it off the sheet, roll the dice, and add the number that's printed there.

Which means the reverse is also true: if it isn't on the sheet, it isn't true at the table. An imagined backstory with no sheet behind it can't swing a sword, cast a spell or take a hit.

No sheet, no character.Filling it in is the moment your character starts existing.

An example of play Part 1 of 5 · The scene

Mira is the Dungeon Master. Tomas plays Brannoch, a Dwarf Fighter. Ines plays Wren, an Elf Rogue. Kofi plays Sable, a Tiefling Wizard. They have just reached a ruined watchtower.

Mira (DM): The tower door hangs open on one hinge. Inside, a stone stair winds up into the dark, and the air smells of wet ash. At the foot of the stairs there is a wooden chest bound in iron. What do you do?

Tomas (Brannoch): (in a gruff voice) “Chests are for opening.” I walk over and grab the lid.

Ines (Wren): Wait, wait. Wren grabs his sleeve. “Brannoch, nobody leaves a chest sitting at the foot of the stairs for free.” Can I check it for traps before he touches it?

Mira: Good instinct. Spotting a hidden trap is a Wisdom check, because you're using your Perception. Disarming it afterward would be a different roll. Start with Perception.

02 · The top strip

Who you are

The top strip: six fields that decide most of the rest of the page. Full character-creation walkthrough: chapter 2 p. 33

  • Character Name — what people call you at the table.
  • Species — what you are: Elf, Dwarf, Goliath. Comes with innate traits. p. 186
  • Background — who you were before adventuring. Gives skills, tools, a feat. p. 177
  • Class — what you do. The single biggest choice on the sheet. ch. 3, p. 48
  • Subclass — your specialisation within that class. ch. 3, p. 48
  • Level & XP — how far along you are, 1 to 20. Everything scales off it. p. 41

03 · The engine

Your six numbers

Ability scores, saving throws and skills — the engine behind almost every roll.

  • Six abilities. Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom, Charisma. p. 10

One ability, close up

  • Score vs. modifier. The big circle holds the modifier; the small box holds the score you actually add. A 14 gives +2. p. 38
  • Saving throws. Rolled when something happens to you — poison, a fireball, a charm. p. 11
  • Skills nest underneath. Stealth sits under Dexterity, Perception under Wisdom, because that's the ability they use. p. 14
  • A filled circle means trained. Add your Proficiency Bonus on top — +2 at level 1, rising to +6. p. 13

Every check, save and skill roll is the same sum

d20  +  ability modifier  +  proficiency bonus (if trained)

Try one

+3MOD
Press the button.

Three ways to generate the six scores

The book gives these three. Pick one before you assign them. p. 38

Standard Array

Take the set 15, 14, 13, 12, 10, 8 and assign them where you like.

Random Generation

Roll four d6 and keep the highest three. Do that six times.

Point Cost

27 points to spend across the six. A 14 costs 7 of them.

Agree it at the table

These are the book's methods, and tables use plenty of others: rolling each score in order with no choice about where it goes, sharing one set of rolls between every player, rolling extra dice and keeping the best three, or giving everyone a bigger point pool. Most of these are house rules, and any of them can be a good fit.

What matters is that everyone at the table uses the same method, and that you settle it before anyone rolls. Talk it through at session zero and let the DM make the final call. If one player rolls well while another buys points, their characters start out uneven, and that tends to cause friction later.

Open the Ability Scores tool to roll or assign them. It can change the number of dice and the size of the point pool, so it can match whatever your table agrees on.

An example of play Part 2 of 5 · The roll

Mira (DM), Tomas (Brannoch), Ines (Wren), Kofi (Sable).

Ines: Okay, so I roll a d20, and then I add... (trailing off, flipping through her sheet) ...my Wisdom, I think? And something about being trained? I know there's a second number in there somewhere.

Mira: It's the same sum every time. Roll a d20, add the ability modifier, and add your proficiency bonus if you're trained in the skill. Find Perception on your sheet. Is the circle beside it filled in?

Ines: It is! I rolled a 9. Wisdom modifier is +1, proficiency bonus is +2. That's 12.

Mira: That's enough. You notice a thin wire running from the lid down into the chest. Someone has rigged it to a small bell. Brannoch, your fingers are half an inch from the lid.

Tomas: (laughing) Brannoch pulls his hand back like the chest bit him. “I knew that.”

Ines: I'd like to cut the wire. That's a Dexterity check with my Thieves' Tools, right?

Mira: Right. You have proficiency with those tools, so you add it.

Ines: I rolled a 14, plus 3 for Dexterity, plus 2 for the tools. That's 19.

Mira: Snip. The wire falls slack. Inside you find a silver ring, a handful of coins, and a folded note. The note reads, “Do not climb.” Almost as soon as you read it, boots clatter on the stairs above. Four goblins come tumbling down. Everybody roll initiative.

04 · Where you come from

Species and background

Your origin — the two fields that fill in the sheet before your class touches it.

Species — what you are p. 177

  • Creature Type. Every playable species in the book is Humanoid.
  • Size. Small or Medium. A few species let you choose.
  • Speed. How far you move on your turn. Usually 30 feet.
  • Special Traits. The distinctive ones — Darkvision, a breath weapon, resistance to a damage type. all ten species: p. 186

On the sheet: SPECIES in the top strip, the SIZE and SPEED boxes, and the traits themselves in SPECIES TRAITS.

Background — who you were p. 177

  • An Origin feat. One, chosen for you by the background. p. 200
  • Two skill proficiencies. Named by the background, not chosen.
  • One tool proficiency. Thieves' Tools, a Forgery Kit, an instrument.
  • Starting equipment. A ready-made kit, or a pile of gold instead. all sixteen backgrounds: p. 178

On the sheet: BACKGROUND in the top strip, filled circles beside the two skills, the tool under EQUIPMENT TRAINING, the feat in FEATS.

House rule

Ability increases are independent of your background. Ignore the three abilities your background lists. At character creation you choose freely: raise two abilities by +2 and +1, or raise three abilities by +1. Increases from levelling up and from feats work exactly as printed.

05 · What you do

How a class works

Every class in chapter 3 is laid out the same way: a traits box, then a table with one row per level.

  • Start with the Core Traits box. Every class opens with one. It tells you your primary ability, your Hit Point die, which two saving throws you're proficient in, the skills you may choose, what weapons and armour you're trained in, and your starting equipment. Copy it onto the sheet before anything else. ch. 3, p. 48
How the book lays it out CORE CLASS TRAITS
Primary Ability · Hit Point Die · Saving Throw Proficiencies
Skill Proficiencies · Weapon Proficiencies
Armor Training · Starting Equipment
→ all of this goes onto the sheet at level 1
  • Then read your row in the features table. One row per level, 1 to 20. Your row gives your Proficiency Bonus and the features you gain. Extra columns hold whatever the class counts — Rages, Sneak Attack dice, spell slots.
LevelProf.Class Features
1+2your opening features
2+2another feature
3+2Subclass
4+2Ability Score Improvement
……up to level 20

The shape is the same for all twelve classes — only the contents change.

  • At level 3 you pick a subclass. All twelve classes work this way. From then on your subclass adds its own features at set levels.
  • Only write down what you have now. At level 1 the rest of the table isn't yours yet. Write the level-1 feature names into CLASS FEATURES, then read each one and make any choice it offers. p. 40

06 · Growing

Levelling up: feats and ability score increases

XP takes you up a level; a handful of those levels hand you a feat.

Twenty levels

a feat subclass class extra
Select a level. At 4, 8, 12 and 16 it is the Ability Score Improvement feat or any other you qualify for; at 19 it is an Epic Boon feat. Level 3: every class picks its subclass.

What a feat level actually gives you

  • It is a feat, not an automatic +1. You may take the Ability Score Improvement feat, or any other feat you qualify for. ch. 5, p. 198
  • The ASI feat itself. Raise one ability by 2, or two abilities by 1 each. It can't push a score above 20, and you can take it again later. p. 202
  • Fighters and Rogues get extras. Fighters also at 6 and 14; Rogues also at 10. p. 92 · p. 130
  • Level 19 is different. An Epic Boon feat instead — the capstone tier. p. 210

Recording a new level on the sheet p. 42

  1. 1 · Choose a class. Usually the same one you already have.
  2. 2 · Hit Points and Hit Dice. Add a die, roll it and add your Con modifier, or take the fixed value.
  3. 3 · Record new class features from your class's table, and make any choices they offer.
  4. 4 · Adjust Proficiency Bonus if it went up, update every number on the sheet built from it.
  5. 5 · Adjust ability modifiers if a feat pushed a score to an even number, redo everything using it.

07 · The big boxes

Where it all gets written down

Three boxes on the sheet hold everything your class, species and background gave you, and a fourth holds your training.

Class Features

What your class hands you level by level — Rage, Sneak Attack, Channel Divinity. The biggest box on the sheet, and the one you'll reread most. ch. 3, p. 48

Species Traits

Things you were born with: darkvision, a breath weapon, resistance to a damage type. p. 186

Feats

Sharp, specific upgrades — one from your background, more at the feat levels. ch. 5, p. 198 origin feats p. 200 general feats p. 202

Equipment Training

Which armour, weapons and tools you were taught to use. Wearing armour you aren't trained in hurts. p. 36 armour p. 219

08 · When the swords come out

Staying alive, and fighting back

The numbers that come out the moment a sword is drawn.

  • Armor Class (AC). How hard you are to hit. An enemy's attack roll has to meet or beat it. With no armour it's 10 + your Dexterity modifier. p. 12 armour table p. 219
  • Hit Points. Damage you can absorb before you drop. Track Current against Max. p. 27
  • Hit Dice. Your self-repair kit — spend them on a short rest to get HP back. p. 41
  • Death Saves. At 0 HP you roll each turn: three successes and you stabilise, three failures and you don't. p. 28
  • Initiative, Speed, Size. Turn order, how far you move, how much space you take up. p. 23 Passive Perception p. 40

Filling the Atk Bonus and Damage columns p. 41

Melee attack bonus  = Strength modifier + proficiency bonus
Ranged attack bonus = Dexterity modifier + proficiency bonus
Damage  = the weapon's dice + that same ability modifier

Proficiency is added to the attack roll, never to the damage roll.

An example of play Part 3 of 5 · Turn order

Mira (DM), Tomas (Brannoch), Ines (Wren), Kofi (Sable).

Kofi (Sable): Is that a d20 plus my Dexterity modifier?

Mira: Yes. That's all you add. Everyone tell me your total and write it in the Initiative box.

Ines: I rolled an 18, plus 3 for Dexterity. That's 21.

Tomas: 11, plus 1. So 12.

Kofi: 8, plus 2. That's 10.

Mira: The goblins get 14. So the order is Wren, then the goblins, then Brannoch, then Sable. Wren, you're first. The lead goblin is on the third step. What do you do?

Ines: “Sorry, friend.” Wren shoots it with her shortbow. I rolled a 12 on the d20, plus 3 for Dexterity and 2 for proficiency, so 17. Does that hit?

Mira: What's its Armor Class? That one's mine to say: it's 15. So yes. Roll your damage.

Ines: A d6 came up 4. Plus 3 for Dexterity, plus 2 for proficiency, so 9 damage?

Mira: Close. Proficiency goes on the attack roll only, never on the damage roll. That's 4, plus 3, so 7. The goblin tumbles back down the stairs and doesn't move.

An example of play Part 4 of 5 · Taking a hit, and missing

Mira: Now the goblins act. One of them lunges at Sable with a rusty blade. Sable, does a 13 hit?

Kofi: My Armor Class is 12, so yes, it hits.

Mira: Then it does 4 damage.

Kofi: (wincing) Ow. That was new robes. I started with 8 hit points, so I'm down to 4. I'll write that in Current Hit Points.

Mira: A second goblin swings at Brannoch. Brannoch, does an 11 hit?

Tomas: My Armor Class is 16, from the chain mail. So it misses.

Mira: The blade glances off your shoulder plate with a screech. The other two are right behind it, bunched up on the stairs. Brannoch, you're up.

Tomas: “Behind me, spellbook!” Brannoch steps in front of Sable and swings his greataxe at the goblin who just hit her. I rolled a 15 on the d20, plus 3 for Strength, plus 2 for proficiency. That's 20.

Mira: That hits. Roll damage.

Tomas: A d12 came up 7. Plus 3, plus 2, so 12.

Mira: Remember, proficiency isn't added to damage. That's 10. A goblin has 7 hit points, so it's down.

09 · Page two

If you cast spells

Page two of the sheet exists only for casters — Fighters and Rogues can skip it.

The two numbers the sheet asks you to work out p. 41

Spell save DC    = 8 + spellcasting ability modifier + proficiency bonus
Spell attack bonus = spellcasting ability modifier + proficiency bonus
  • Spellcasting Ability. Which of the six powers your magic: Intelligence for Wizards, Wisdom for Clerics and Druids, Charisma for Bards, Sorcerers and Warlocks. ch. 7, p. 235
  • Spell Slots. Your fuel. Casting spends a slot of that spell's level; a long rest gives them all back. p. 235
  • Cantrips are free. Unlimited, no slot spent. Prepared spells are the list you chose that morning. p. 235 spell descriptions p. 239

How to read a spell

Every entry in chapter 7 is laid out exactly this way. The spell below is Burning Hands, p. 248

BURNING HANDS

Level 1 Evocation (Sorcerer, Wizard)

Casting Time: Action

Range: Self

Components: V, S

Duration: Instantaneous

A thin sheet of flame shoots from your fingertips. Every creature in a 15-foot Cone makes a Dexterity saving throw, taking 3d6 Fire damage on a failure, half as much on a success.

Using a Higher-Level Spell Slot. The damage increases by 1d6 for each slot level above 1.

Burning Hands, Player's Handbook p. 248 — effect text condensed.

  • Level and school. The level tells you which slot it costs — a level 1 spell fits any slot. The school is mostly flavour. The names in brackets are the class lists it appears on. p. 235 p. 236
  • Casting Time. Usually an Action. Some spells take a Bonus Action or a Reaction with a stated trigger; a few take minutes, and those need Concentration while you cast. p. 236
  • Range. A distance, Touch, or Self. Self doesn't mean it only affects you — here the cone starts at your hands. p. 236
  • Components. V is spoken, S needs a free hand, M needs a material. A component pouch or spellcasting focus covers most M. p. 237
  • Duration. Instantaneous, a time span, or Concentration — and you can only concentrate on one spell at a time. p. 237
  • The effect. Who it hits, what they roll, what it does. This one asks for a saving throw against your spell save DC; others ask you for a spell attack roll instead. p. 237 p. 238
  • Using a Higher-Level Spell Slot. Spend a bigger slot and the spell gets bigger. Not every spell has this line. p. 236

11 · Preparing spells

Prepared spells, “known” spells, and Pact Magic

The 2024 rules folded “spells known” into prepared spells. Everyone prepares now — what differs is when you may change the list. p. 235

ClassYou may change the list when you…How many
Bardgain a levelone
Clericfinish a Long Restany
Druidfinish a Long Restany
Paladinfinish a Long Restone
Rangerfinish a Long Restone
Sorcerergain a levelone
Warlockgain a levelone
Wizardfinish a Long Restany

Cantrips sit outside all of this — always available, never a slot. Features that grant an “always-prepared” spell don't count against these numbers either.

So there are really two patterns

  • Swap on a Long Rest. Cleric, Druid and Wizard rebuild the list every morning; Paladin and Ranger change one spell. Flexible — retune for the day ahead.
  • Swap on level up. Bard, Sorcerer and Warlock are locked in between levels, changing one spell each time they level. Choose for versatility, not for one encounter.
  • The Wizard is the odd one out. It prepares from its spellbook rather than the whole class list, so copying new spells into the book is how a Wizard grows. p. 166
Pact Magic · the Warlock runs a different engine p. 153

Very few slots, all the same level — and that level climbs with you. At Warlock 5 you have two level 3 slots, and anything you cast with one is cast at level 3.

They come back on a Short Rest, not only a Long one, so a Warlock casts less per fight but more per day.

Pact slots stop at level 5. Levels 6 to 9 arrive as Mystic Arcanum instead — one spell of each level, once per Long Rest.

An example of play Part 5 of 5 · The spell

Mira (DM), Tomas (Brannoch), Ines (Wren), Kofi (Sable).

Mira: Sable, your turn. The last two goblins are standing close together on the stairs.

Kofi: I'd like to cast Burning Hands. Do I roll to hit them?

Mira: Not with this spell. Burning Hands doesn't use an attack roll. Each creature in the cone makes a Dexterity saving throw against your spell save DC, so they roll, not you. What's your DC?

Kofi: It's 8, plus my Intelligence modifier of +3, plus my proficiency bonus of +2. That's 13.

Mira: Right. Don't forget the spell uses up a level 1 slot. How many do you have?

Kofi: Two. So I cross one off, and I have one left until a long rest.

Mira: Good. Roll your damage.

Kofi: Sable's hands blaze. “Burn, you little pests!” I rolled 3d6 and got 11.

Mira: One goblin rolls a 6 and fails its save, so it takes all 11 and collapses. The other rolls a 16 and succeeds, so it takes half of 11, which is 5, rounded down. It has 2 hit points left. It squeals, drops its blade, and bolts back up the stairs.

12 · The epilogue

Multiclassing

Taking your next level in a second class instead of your first. Chapter 2 p. 44

What changes, and what doesn't

  • You need a 13. To add a class you need at least 13 in the primary ability of the new class and of every class you already have.
  • XP and Proficiency Bonus follow your total level. A level 6 Cleric / level 1 Fighter is a level 7 character, with a level 7 character's proficiency bonus.
  • Hit Points. You only get a class's generous level-1 hit points if your total character level is 1. After that it's the ordinary per-level amount, and your Hit Dice pool mixes die types.
  • Proficiencies are trimmed. A new class gives you only part of its starting proficiencies. The class entry says which.
  • Some things never stack. Extra Attack doesn't add up, and if two classes offer a way to work out your AC you pick one.

Spellcasting across two classes p. 45

  • Prepare separately. Work out each class's prepared spells as if you were single-classed in it.
  • But share one pool of slots. Add up your spellcasting levels, look that total up on the Multiclass Spellcaster table, and use those slots for any of your prepared spells.
  • Slots can outrun your spells. You may hold a level 4 slot with nothing of level 4 prepared. Use it to cast a lower-level spell at a higher level.
  • Pact Magic mixes freely. A Warlock/caster can spend Pact slots on prepared Spellcasting spells and ordinary slots on Warlock spells.

Working out your spell slots when you multiclass p. 45

full levels in Bard, Cleric, Druid, Sorcerer, Wizard
+ half your Paladin and Ranger levels, rounded up
+ a third of Eldritch Knight or Arcane Trickster levels, rounded down
Look the total up on the Multiclass Spellcaster table.

Before you do it: every level in a second class is a level your first class isn't getting. Subclass features, Extra Attack and higher spell slots all arrive later. Ask your DM first.
